Changelog

What shipped, and when.

Each entry says what changed for you. Where something needs a key, a plan or a setting that is not on for everyone, the entry says so. For how each part works today, read the docs; for what is planned, the roadmap.

  1. 25 October 2026

    Offboarding that removes everywhere, and reviews an auditor can follow

    A leaver's approved run can remove them through each app's own API, and reviews gained stages, schedules and richer evidence.

    • Offboarding: when an admin switches it on, an approved leaver's run removes the person through each supported app's own API at once, and each removal is read back to prove it. It is off by default, and the approval shows how many apps it will touch.
    • Reviews can have one to three stages, for example manager then app owner. Stage two sees stage one's decision and reason.
    • Review schedules can recur every N days, run as delta reviews (only what changed), and end on the due date. They launch on the server with nobody in the app.
    • Revoking privileged access removes every path to it, whether direct, through a group or through a role, and shows a preview first.
    • A manual removal can carry up to three screenshots or PDFs and a ticket link. They go into the evidence pack unchanged, with their hashes.
    • When you sign off a review or finish an offboarding, the evidence can be pushed to Vanta or Drata automatically once you connect either one.
    • Evidence now warns before it expires, and control tests run across six frameworks with a CSV per framework in the pack.
    • Custom roles with read, edit and act permissions per area, and a stream of Proof to S3, Splunk, Datadog or a signed webhook in OCSF form.
    • Jira and ServiceNow: push tasks as tickets, map fields and keep the ticket as evidence.
  2. 25 October 2026

    Claude can work your access queue, and AI hooks cover more of your agents

    Truehound's MCP server gained sign-in, more tools and tokens that expire, and agents got their own identities.

    • Sign in to Truehound from Claude and other MCP clients with OAuth 2.1: a consent page, short-lived access tokens, and refresh tokens that rotate.
    • Claude can now approve and deny access requests, decide review rows, start a review and list unused licenses, as the person whose token it uses. Every write still goes through your approval policy, and nobody can approve their own request.
    • Tokens can be read only and can expire after 1 to 365 days. An admin sets token lifetimes and the client hosts that are allowed.
    • MCP hook policies can be drafted from your last 30 days of history, by Claude on your own Anthropic key or by rules, with each rule's impact shown first.
    • AI agents get short-lived tokens of their own, and a library of agents with a builder for your own.
  3. 25 October 2026

    Roles, access paths and policies

    More ways to see and control who can reach what.

    • Ask who can reach a resource with a query that combines AND, OR and NOT, across nested groups, and save the query or scope a review with it. It is also in the API and over MCP.
    • Access policies allow-list the roles each app should have, show a Deviations view with severity, and alert the app's owners about new ones.
    • Change a person to any role an app's API can set, and read the app again at the new role.
    • Machine identities, service accounts and API keys are ranked by risk across seven live sources, with revoke and owners.
    • A server workflow engine with branches, waits, parallel paths and versioned flows.
    • Identity matching rules, overrides and source priority you can configure, and a person's type taken from HR and identity data with an override.
  4. 24 October 2026

    Autopilot and your own AI key

    The AI now does the routine access work inside your rules, and runs only on a key you own.

    • Autopilot works the access queue: it triages findings, removes stale and risky access, grants routine requests for a set time, pre-fills reviews, and runs leavers end to end. Each job is off, prepare for approval, or act alone. A kill switch stops everything, and it never grants an admin role.
    • Bring your own Anthropic key. Every AI feature runs on the key an admin adds, so the charges go to your own account, and without a key everything runs on rules and is labelled as rules.
    • Govern the tools your AI coding agents call with a small open hook for Claude Code, Cursor, Windsurf, VS Code Copilot and Codex. It allows, asks or denies each call before it runs.
    • AI spend by person, team and model.
    • Scheduled GitHub scans from a stored read-only token, proven on production with a real 15-minute cron run.
    • Hands-off seat reclaim under two admins' standing approval, and HR systems HiBob, Personio, Gusto, Workday and Kombo that mark leavers.
    • Device checks from Intune, Jamf Pro and Kandji: screen lock, malware protection and firewall per person, with unknown never counted as a pass.
  5. 23 October 2026

    Posture, workflows and a page for every employee

    Truehound looks past accounts to how access is configured, and employees see their own.

    • A posture page for privileged access, access paths, non-human identities, sign-in methods and integration health.
    • A workflow builder, birthright access templates with a rule simulator, and apps provisioned through identity provider groups.
    • Connection health with sync history and an alert when an app stops authenticating, change history on configuration, and an export of every admin action.
    • My access: every employee sees their own accounts, the policies they accepted, and imported security training.
    • An inventory of the AI keys and agents people create, a SCIM bridge for apps that have no SCIM, and connector drafts from an OpenAPI file.
    • Seat limits with reclaim or buy, the employee App hub, app criticality tiers, imported payments with spend by month, and cost allocation.
    • Separation-of-duties rules, SSO coverage per app, and evidence expiry in reviews.
  6. 22 October 2026

    A public API, Terraform, access requests and review campaigns

    Truehound became a platform you can script, with requests, reviews and discovery.

    • A public REST API with scoped keys and signed webhooks, a Terraform provider for review schedules, the approval policy, role bundles and webhooks, SAML and OIDC sign-in, and no-code custom connectors.
    • Access requests with approval, grant and a read-back, time-bound access that ends on its own, joiners and movers, SCIM in both directions, and bulk offboarding with hand-overs.
    • Review campaigns with many reviewers, schedules, delta reviews, accepted risk, and evidence for your auditor.
    • Discovery of the apps people use, from OAuth grants, sign-in logs, card spend, email metadata and a browser extension.
    • Free tools, guides and comparison pages, now published at real addresses so search engines can read them.
    • A privacy policy, data processing agreement, sub-processor list and Trust Center, each checked line by line against the code.
  7. 21 October 2026

    Every app, not just GitHub

    Truehound reads who has access across your company, with a leavers board, a review and an evidence pack.

    • A framework for connecting apps, starting with Google Workspace, Slack and PagerDuty, and growing that day to include Microsoft Entra ID, Okta, Rippling, BambooHR, Atlassian, Notion, Datadog, AWS IAM and Google Cloud IAM, each tested against the vendor's documented responses.
    • One inventory of people across tools, a standing Left, still has access board, an access review, an evidence ZIP and an offboarding receipt.
    • The free scan: your GitHub organization, the real score and counts, names hidden, with no account.
    • Match suggestions for accounts with no email, each with its reasons, that a person confirms.
    • Apps you cannot read show as Couldn't check and are never counted as clean. A scan that GitHub's rate limit cut short is never saved as if it were complete.
    • Secrets in code, found through GitHub code search on your default branches and shown masked.
    • Ask Truehound, an AI investigator grounded in your scan.
  8. 20 October 2026

    Truehound Access Audit

    Who still has the keys to your GitHub org, on one page, with one-click fixes.

    • Paste or upload the people who left, connect GitHub with a token, and see who can still reach your code: departed people still in the org, outside collaborators, owners, old deploy keys, webhooks to unknown hosts, public repos and secrets in code.
    • Four fixes from the app, each only after you confirm: remove a member, demote an owner to member, remove an outside collaborator, delete a deploy key.
    • A report in Markdown or HTML, Save as PDF, and a share-your-score image.
    • Team workspaces with roles, invites, and fixes that follow an approval policy, with an admin override that needs a reason.
    • A GitHub offboarding checklist anyone can use.
