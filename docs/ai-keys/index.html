AI

AI keys (bring your own key)

Last compared with the product's code 25 October 2026.

Every AI feature in Truehound runs on your own Anthropic key. An admin pastes the key once, switches AI on, and the charges go to your own Anthropic account. If you never add a key, nothing is sent to Anthropic and everything works from rules.

What you need, and what is not on yet

AI is off by default. Truehound has no key of its own for customers, and pays nothing for your AI. Without your key every feature runs its rules and is labelled as rules. The key and the opt-in are per workspace and need a team workspace. Provider inventory of AI keys covers OpenAI, Anthropic and GitHub Copilot.

Add your Anthropic key

  1. Step 1. Create a key in your Anthropic account

    Use a key made for Truehound so you can see its usage and revoke it on its own.

  2. Step 2. Paste it under Settings, AI

    Truehound proves the key works with one call that lists models, which costs nothing, then stores it encrypted (AES-256-GCM, under a key derived for your workspace). Only its last four characters are ever shown again.

  3. Step 3. Opt in

    A consent screen lists exactly what is sent to Anthropic. One switch turns AI on for every AI use in the workspace. You can turn it off again at any time.

What it does to your data

  • Only the facts a feature needs are sent: for review suggestions, each row's app, name, email, roles, status, last sign-in, and admin, two-step and guest flags. The consent screen lists the rest.
  • The key is never in a response, a log, an export or an audit line. You can replace or remove it, and each change is sealed in Proof with who made it and the last four characters, never the key.
  • Anthropic's commercial terms bar training on customer content (Anthropic commercial terms, Customer Content).
  • A usage meter shows calls and tokens this month, taken from Anthropic's own responses. There is an hourly rate limit per workspace.

What turns on

With a key and the opt-in: reasons on review lines, the review and approval step, request decisions inside your rules, guided fixes, the campaign builder, the assistant, scheduled agents, and Claude on Autopilot and in drafted MCP hook policies.

Without a key, each of those runs its rules, labelled as rules, and the app shows an empty state that tells you to add a key.

AI keys and agents, the inventory

The AI keys and agents page is a different thing: it is the inventory of AI keys other people created. It reads every AI API key, project, service account and Copilot seat from the providers' admin APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic and GitHub Copilot), with its owner and its last use where the provider reports one.

A revoke there counts only when a fresh read agrees. Beside the keys it shows the AI agents and connections Truehound already sees: MCP servers and agent tokens, sign-ins to AI apps reported by the browser extension, AI bots in connected apps, and OAuth grants to AI apps from Google and Microsoft.

When a person leaves, the agents, tokens and apps they created can be offboarded with them. The live demo shows this page on a fictional company.

AI spend

Spend by person, team and model, with budgets per workspace and team, a month-end forecast and spike alerts, is on the same page.
