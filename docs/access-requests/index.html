Access requests

Last compared with the product's code 25 October 2026.

An access request is a short form: which app, which role, why, and for how long. The right person approves or denies with a reason. Truehound grants the access through the app and checks it, or hands the work to the app's owner as a task. Every step is recorded in Proof.

What you need, and what is not on yet

Access requests need a team workspace and, for each app, a connection that can grant access. Requests and approvals inside Slack and Microsoft Teams are built but not switched on yet, because the Slack app and the Teams bot are not registered. Email approvals need email sending, which is not on yet. Approving in Truehound works today.

Make an app requestable

On the Access requests page an admin picks which connected apps people may request, which roles, and who approves: the owner, the manager, or the manager and then the owner. The approval policy sets how many people approve, and an admin role needs more.

An app with no owner says so in plain words, and the request goes to an admin.

Request, approve, grant

  1. Step 1. The employee asks

    They choose the app and role, say why, and say for how long. The request shows the seat cost, any unused seats that could be reused instead, and the department's budget impact.

  2. Step 2. The approver decides

    An approver approves or denies in Truehound or through the API. Once the Slack app is registered, they can do it in Slack too. A denial must carry a reason, and the requester sees it. Separation-of-duties rules are enforced at request time.

  3. Step 3. Truehound grants and checks

    It grants the access through the app's API, SCIM, or the identity provider's groups, then reads the app again before calling it granted. If the app has no API, the request becomes a task for the owner with evidence.

  4. Step 4. Time-bound access ends on time

    Access with an end date counts down. At expiry, the server removes it, restores the role the person had before, and reads the app again. The requester can end it early.

More ways in

  • The employee App hub lists apps people can request and shows their own access and who administers each app.
  • Claude, through the MCP server, and the API can list, request, approve, deny and cancel requests as the person whose token is used, under the same approval policy and with the same rule that nobody approves their own request.
  • Tasks for an app's owner can be pushed as tickets to Jira or ServiceNow, with field mapping and the ticket kept as evidence.
  • When an app has no seats left, the request offers reclaiming an unused one or buying another.

Let Autopilot handle the routine ones

The Autopilot grant job can approve routine requests inside rules you set, for a set time, and send unusual ones to a person with its reasons. See Autopilot.
