Getting started

Last compared with the product's code 25 October 2026.

Truehound is an AI access agent. It finds who has access to your apps, removes what should be gone, and proves each removal by reading the app again. Start by looking at your own GitHub organization for nothing, or open the demo, then connect your apps and run one review.

What you need, and what is not on yet

The free scan and the live demo need no account. Everything else needs a Truehound workspace on a plan. Email sign-in links are not switched on yet: you sign in by payment, an activation code, an invite link or a passkey.

Try it before you buy

There are two ways in that ask for nothing. Neither one needs an account.

  • The free scan reads your GitHub organization with a token you create. It shows the real score and counts, with names hidden, and finds former staff, outside admins, old deploy keys, public repos and secrets in code.
  • The live demo is the whole product on a made-up company. Nothing you do in it touches a real app. There are also click-through tours, one per task.

Set up in this order

  1. Step 1. Open a workspace

    Pick a plan on the pricing page. The price is flat and never per seat. After you pay, the page Stripe returns you to signs you in.

    A teammate joins by an invite link from an admin. Later, a passkey saved on your device signs you back in.

  2. Step 2. Connect your identity source and your HR system

    Connect the system that says who is a current employee first. Truehound takes the leaving date from your HR system when you have one, and uses your identity provider to confirm it. If a person is suspended in the identity provider but still active in other apps, that is a finding.

    See Connect your apps for each way an app can be connected.

  3. Step 3. Connect the apps that hold your data

    Add your code host, cloud accounts and the apps your team uses. Each app has a card on the Sources page that says what Truehound can read, whether it can remove people through that app's own API, and which plan the vendor needs for that.

  4. Step 4. Read the people list and the Left, still has access board

    People shows one row per person across every app, with each account, role and last sign-in. Accounts that match no person are listed under No person for you to resolve.

    The Left, still has access board lists everyone who has left and still holds an active account somewhere, worst first, with a clock from the day they left.

  5. Step 5. Run your first review and keep the proof

    Start an access review, decide every line, sign off, and download the evidence pack. Each change is sealed in Proof, a hash chain you can verify yourself.

Where things live

  • Sources: connect, reconnect and check each app.
  • People and Left, still has access: who holds what, and who should not.
  • Access review and Review campaigns: run, schedule and sign off reviews.
  • Access requests and Joiners and leavers: self-serve access, and the offboarding runs.
  • Autopilot: the AI jobs and their switches.
  • AI keys and agents: the AI keys and agents people created, and MCP hooks.
  • Proof: every action, verifiable.
  • Settings: your Anthropic key, roles, approvals and the developer tools.

What Truehound will not do

  • It never calls someone departed unless you list them or your HR system or identity provider says so.
  • It never shows a check it could not run as a pass. Apps it could not read are listed as not checked, with the reason.
  • It counts a removal as done only when it has read the app again and the access is gone. A removal you did by hand is labelled manual and is not counted as verified.
