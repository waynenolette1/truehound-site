How fast must you remove a terminated employee's access for SOC 2?

Sources checked 22 October 2026. 5 minute read.

The short answer

SOC 2 does not set a number. The logical access criteria (CC6.x) expect access to be removed when someone is no longer authorized, and your own policy says how fast. The auditor then tests every sampled leaver against the window you wrote down. Common policies say same day, 24 hours, one business day or 48 hours; pick one you can meet every time, because missing your own deadline is the exception.

SOC 2 sets no fixed deadline

SOC 2 reports are built on the AICPA Trust Services Criteria. The logical access criteria, CC6.x, cover granting access, removing it when people leave, and limiting it to what a role needs. They describe the outcome, not a number of hours.

So the deadline is yours. Practitioners put it plainly: set a termination period your organization can achieve, because the auditor tests the control against the requirement you define. Write the window into your logical access policy as well as the control.[1],[2]

Common windows companies choose

  • Same day: the strictest choice. Only promise it if your process really runs that fast, every time.
  • 24 hours: a widely used example reads that user access is revoked within 24 hours of the employee record being terminated in the HR system.
  • One business day: another common wording is that access no longer required for terminated or transferred users is removed within one business day. Bastion describes the typical window as 24 hours or one business day.
  • 48 hours: Strike Graph's default control language says access is revoked within 48 hours of termination or transfer, and tells you to customise it. If you always remove access within 24 hours, write 24.[3],[2],[4]

A shorter window is not better if you miss it. A late removal in the sample is an exception, while a longer window you always meet is not.

What the auditor samples

  1. Step 1. The population of leavers[2],[6]

    For a Type 2 report, expect a request for a termination list for the audit period from your HR or payroll system. The auditor picks a sample from it.

  2. Step 2. Proof of removal for each sampled leaver[2],[7]

    For each sampled person, the auditor wants a ticket or checklist showing access was removed within your stated interval. One practitioner checklist expects a revocation log covering every system the person held, with timestamps, not just the identity provider.

  3. Step 3. Termination date against removal timestamp[6],[2]

    The test is simple arithmetic: the termination date from HR against the date access ended in each system. A deactivation inside the policy deadline for every sampled leaver is what passes.

  4. Step 4. The written procedure[2]

    Strike Graph also lists your access termination procedure as evidence, including who first notifies IT that access must be removed.

Where teams miss the window

  • Terminations caused 12% of control exceptions across the 193 SOC reports in CBIZ's 2024 benchmark study, behind business approvals and reviews (16.5%) and user access reviews (15.6%).
  • Bastion calls deprovisioning the most frequently cited exception, usually because HR tells IT late or the offboarding checklist is incomplete. Its example: someone resigns on Friday and their AWS and GitHub access is removed the next Tuesday.
  • Apps outside single sign-on or SCIM are hard to track, so leavers keep direct logins there.
  • Contractors often are not in the HR system, so no termination event ever fires for them.
  • Missing evidence: the access was removed, but nobody can show when.[5],[4],[3],[7]

Make the window hold

  1. Step 1. Start the clock from HR[4]

    Connect your HR system to your identity provider where you can, so the termination date starts deprovisioning. Bastion recommends triggering it on the termination date.

  2. Step 2. Keep a per-system checklist[4],[7]

    List every in-scope system, including those people reach with a direct login, and record the removal time for each. The offboarding checklist at /tools/offboarding-checklist builds one for your apps.

  3. Step 3. Reconcile weekly[4]

    Bastion suggests checking recent terminations against access each week, so a miss is caught days after it happens rather than in the audit.

  4. Step 4. Check your readiness

    The readiness quiz at /tools/access-review-readiness shows where your offboarding and access review evidence is thin. For the quarterly review itself, see /guides/user-access-review-template-soc-2.

Questions

Does SOC 2 require removing access within 24 hours?
No. The criteria do not name a number. 24 hours is a common policy choice, and the auditor tests you against whatever window your policy states.
Is a longer window a red flag?
Practitioners advise a window you can achieve. A window you always meet is better than a short one you sometimes miss. Agree the wording with your auditor.
What counts as the start of the clock?
Whatever your policy says, most often the termination date or the moment the HR record is terminated. Write it down so the auditor measures from the same point you do.
Do contractors count?
Yes, if they had access to in-scope systems. They are easy to miss because they are often not in the HR system.
Is removing someone from the identity provider enough?
Only for apps that run through it. Auditors look at every in-scope system, and apps with direct logins are a common gap.
