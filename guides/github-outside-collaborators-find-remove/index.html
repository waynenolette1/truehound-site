How to find and remove GitHub outside collaborators

Sources checked 22 October 2026. 5 minute read.

The short answer

Outside collaborators are people who are not members of your GitHub organization but have been added to one or more of its repositories. List them under People, then Outside collaborators, or with GET /orgs/{org}/outside_collaborators. Remove them from every repository at once from that list, or from a single repository under Manage access. On GitHub Enterprise Cloud you can also stop repository admins from adding new ones.

What an outside collaborator is

An outside collaborator is a person who is not a member of your organization but has access to one or more of its repositories, at an access level you choose per repository. They cannot be added to teams, because teams are only for members.

Base permissions, the default access every member gets, do not apply to outside collaborators. Their access is exactly the set of repositories they were added to, which is why the list is easy to lose track of.[1],[3]

Why contractors end up there, and stay

  • By default, anyone with admin access to a repository can invite outside collaborators to it. Repository admins add an agency or a freelancer to ship one project, and nobody removes them when it ends.
  • GitHub suggests converting a member to an outside collaborator for people who only need some repositories, such as consultants or temporary staff. They lose membership and teams but keep the repositories they were added to directly or through their former teams.
  • Outside collaborators on private repositories use paid licenses on paid plans, so a forgotten contractor also costs a seat.
  • On the People tab they sit in their own list, and GitHub's member export describes member fields only, so a member review can miss them.[2],[4],[1],[11]

List every outside collaborator

  1. Step 1. In organization settings[6],[7]

    Go to your organization, open the People tab, and choose Outside collaborators in the sidebar. Pending collaborators, in the same sidebar, lists invitations that have not been accepted yet.

  2. Step 2. With the REST API[8],[9]

    GET /orgs/{org}/outside_collaborators lists them for the whole organization; add filter=2fa_disabled to see only those without two-factor authentication. For one repository, GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/collaborators?affiliation=outside lists its outside collaborators.

  3. Step 3. Check each one against a person and a reason

    For each collaborator, write down who they are, who at your company sponsors them, which repositories they can reach, and when the work ends. Anyone without a current sponsor is a removal. The template at /tools/access-review-template has columns for this.

Remove from one repository or from all of them

  1. Step 1. From all repositories[6],[8]

    On People, then Outside collaborators, select the people, open the dropdown and choose Remove from all repositories, then confirm with Remove outside collaborators. By API, DELETE /orgs/{org}/outside_collaborators/{username} does the same. It returns 422 if the person is a member, not an outside collaborator.

  2. Step 2. From a single repository[6],[10],[9]

    In the Outside collaborators list, click the gear next to the person, choose Manage, then Manage access next to the repository and Remove access to this repository. From the repository side, Settings, then Collaborators and teams, lists everyone with access and has a Remove button. By API, use DELETE /repos/{owner}/{repo}/collaborators/{username}.

  3. Step 3. Cancel pending invitations[7]

    On People, open Pending collaborators, find the person and cancel the invitation. An unaccepted invite can still be accepted later.

GitHub deletes an outside collaborator's forks of your private repositories when you remove them, but any local clone stays on their machine. Removing them also does not lower your paid license count; downgrade the seats yourself.

Convert a collaborator to a member instead

If the person is joining the company, invite them as a member rather than leaving them as a collaborator. On People, then Outside collaborators, open the menu next to their name and choose Invite to organization, then pick a role and teams. They must accept the email invitation, and on per-seat plans you need an unused license before you can invite them.

As a member they pick up your base permissions and team access, which you can review in one place.[5],[3]

Limit who can add outside collaborators

On GitHub Enterprise Cloud, open organization Settings, then Member privileges, and under Repository outside collaborators clear Allow repository administrators to invite outside collaborators to repositories for this organization. After that, only organization owners can add them. If your organization belongs to an enterprise, an enterprise policy may set this for you.

While you are on Member privileges, check Base permissions. They set the default repository access for every member, from no permission up, and a repository admin can still grant more on a single repository.[2],[3]

Questions

Do outside collaborators count toward my GitHub bill?
On paid plans, adding an outside collaborator to a private repository uses one of your paid licenses.
Why does removing an outside collaborator return 422?
The person is a member, not an outside collaborator. Remove them with the organization members endpoint instead.
Can outside collaborators be added to teams?
No. Team membership is only for organization members. Collaborators are added to repositories one at a time.
Who can add outside collaborators?
By default, anyone with admin access to a repository. On GitHub Enterprise Cloud, owners can restrict this to organization owners only.
Does removing a collaborator delete their copy of the code?
It deletes their forks of your private repositories on GitHub. Local clones on their own machine are not affected.
