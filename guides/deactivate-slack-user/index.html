How to deactivate a user in Slack

Sources checked 21 October 2026. 5 minute read.

The short answer

Workspace Owners and Admins deactivate people from Admin, Workspace settings, People: click the three dots next to the person and choose Deactivate account. Deactivation signs them out on every device, removes them from all channels and keeps their messages and files. Slack accounts cannot be permanently deleted, only deactivated. Check the apps they installed afterwards, because some stop working and their API tokens are revoked.

Who can deactivate an account, by plan

  • Pro, Business+ and Slack's entry plan: Workspace Admins can deactivate members and guests. Owners can also deactivate Admins, and the Primary Owner can deactivate Owners. No one can deactivate the Workspace Primary Owner.
  • Enterprise plans (often still called Enterprise Grid): Org Admins and Owners can deactivate members, guests, and Workspace Admins and Owners. Only the Org Primary Owner can deactivate Org Owners and Admins. People with the Security Admin or Users Admin system role can act on anyone except the Org Primary Owner.[1]

Deactivate a member step by step

  1. Step 1. On a single workspace (Pro, Business+ or the entry plan)[1]

    Click Admin in the sidebar, choose Workspace settings, then People. Click the three dots next to the person and select Deactivate account.

  2. Step 2. On an Enterprise org[1]

    Click your organization name in the sidebar, then Tools and settings, Organization settings, People, Members. Tick the person and click Deactivate above the list.

  3. Step 3. Force sessions to end now (Enterprise, by API)[1],[7]

    Deactivation already signs the person out on all devices. On Enterprise plans you can also call the admin.users.session.reset method with a user token that has the admin.users:write scope. It wipes every valid session for that user on all devices.

What deactivation does to messages, files and sessions

  • Messages and files are not deleted. The person is not notified.
  • They are removed from all channels, signed out of your workspaces on all devices, and cannot sign back in.
  • Profile details such as name and photo stay, so old messages still show who wrote them.
  • Slack accounts cannot be permanently deleted, only deactivated. You can reactivate the account later if you change your mind.[1],[5]

Guests: deactivate or let the time limit do it

Guest accounts exist only on paid plans. Multi-Channel Guests see only the channels you pick and are billed like regular members. Single-Channel Guests can reach one channel.

Admins deactivate guests the same way as members. You can also set a time limit when you invite a guest: Slack deactivates them on that date and reminds the guest and the admin five days before. To keep a guest but narrow what they see, remove them from specific channels instead.[3],[1]

Bots and apps the person installed

When a member is deactivated, some apps they installed deactivate automatically. Bot users, slash commands and incoming webhooks usually keep working, but apps that need that member's permissions may stop completely. Any API tokens the member granted are revoked.

Slack keeps a short list of Slack-built apps running whatever the installer's status. For the rest, open the list of deactivated apps and integrations. Reinstall an app, or click Enable for an integration. Reactivating a bot integration regenerates its token, so update any code that used the old one.

If every collaborator on an internal app has been deactivated, an Org Owner or Workspace Owner must add a new collaborator before the app can be turned back on.[2]

Before deactivating someone who built your integrations, list the apps they installed. A deploy notifier or alert bot can go quiet the moment they are deactivated.

Deactivate with SCIM instead of clicks

If your identity provider manages Slack, deactivate there and let SCIM push the change. Slack's SCIM API is available on Business+ and Enterprise plans. Slack's help center adds that the entry and Pro plans get SCIM provisioning only when a Salesforce org is connected to Slack.

A SCIM call needs an OAuth token with the admin scope, and the account that generated the token must stay an Admin or Owner. DELETE /Users/{id} sets the Slack user to deactivated. A PATCH that sets active to false does the same, and setting it back to true reactivates the user.[5],[6],[4]

Questions

Can I delete a Slack user instead of deactivating them?
No. Slack only deactivates accounts. Their messages and files stay, and you can reactivate the account later.
Is the person told they were deactivated?
No. Slack does not notify people when their account is deactivated. They are signed out on all devices and cannot sign back in.
Who can deactivate a Workspace Owner?
On a single workspace, only the Primary Owner can deactivate Owners, and no one can deactivate the Primary Owner. On Enterprise plans, only the Org Primary Owner can deactivate Org Owners and Admins.
Will our Slack bots break when the person who set them up leaves?
Possibly. Some apps deactivate with the installer and their API tokens are revoked. Bot users, slash commands and incoming webhooks usually survive. Check the deactivated apps list right after offboarding.
Which Slack plans support SCIM deprovisioning?
Business+ and Enterprise. Slack's help center says the entry and Pro plans get SCIM only when a Salesforce org is connected.
