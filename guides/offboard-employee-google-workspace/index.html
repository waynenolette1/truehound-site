How to offboard an employee from Google Workspace

Sources checked 21 October 2026. 6 minute read.

The short answer

Reset the password and sign-in cookies, revoke app passwords and connected apps, then suspend the account so nothing new gets in. Transfer their Drive files and Calendar, and redirect their mail to a manager. Then archive the account (keeps the data and lets you reuse the paid seat) or delete it. Google lets you restore a deleted user for 20 days, so transfer data before you delete.

Suspend, archive or delete: pick the end state first

Google gives you three states for a person who left. They differ in what they cost and what they keep.

  • Suspend: blocks access to Gmail, Drive and other services, keeps all data, and can be undone at any time. Suspended accounts are still charged at the same rate as active ones.
  • Archive: the person cannot sign in anywhere, their data is kept, and their Workspace license becomes available to reassign within 24 hours. Business and Enterprise editions usually need an Archived User subscription (Flexible Plan customers can archive without one); archived data counts toward your pooled storage.
  • Delete: the person loses access to all services and data. Data you did not transfer is removed. You can restore the account for 20 days, after that it is gone.[2],[3],[1]

Google's own advice: do not delete just to block access (suspend instead) or just to save a license (archive instead).

Cut access on the last day

  1. Step 1. Reset the password and the sign-in cookies[5],[6]

    In the Admin console go to Directory, then Users, and open the person's account page. Under Security, reset the password.

    Then open Security, Sign-in cookies, and click Reset. This signs them out of browsers and mobile devices. It does not sign them out of apps such as Gmail and Google Drive for desktop, which is one more reason to suspend.

  2. Step 2. Revoke app passwords and connected apps[6]

    On the same Security page, open Application-specific passwords and revoke each one. Older mail clients and scripts use these to sign in without the normal password.

    Then open Connected applications. It lists the third-party apps that can reach the person's data. Click Remove on each. Google notes that removing access does not stop the person from using the app later, so do this together with the suspension.

  3. Step 3. Wipe work data from managed phones[10]

    If you manage their phone, open their account page, click Managed devices, and choose Wipe Account. That removes the work account and leaves personal data. Wipe Device resets the whole phone and is meant for lost or stolen devices.

    An account wipe only works on devices under basic or advanced mobile management. Google says the user must still be able to reach their work account, so do this before you suspend, or restore the account briefly first.

  4. Step 4. Remove admin roles and group memberships[1],[8]

    Remove any admin role first. Google will not delete a user who still holds one.

    In Directory, Groups, open each group, click Members, point to the person and click Remove. They stop getting group mail; the account itself stays until you deal with it.

  5. Step 5. Suspend the account[2],[5]

    In Directory, Users, point to the person and suspend them. Suspension blocks new mail and calendar invitations, keeps every file, and resets their sign-in cookies automatically.

Hand over Drive, Calendar and email

  1. Step 1. Transfer Drive files to a manager[7],[1]

    In the Admin console go to Apps, Google Workspace, Drive and Docs, then Transfer ownership. Pick the From and To users and click Transfer Files. You need the Drive and Docs administrator privilege.

    Before you start: move anything out of Trash (Trash is not transferred) and move unorganized files into their My Drive. The new owner must be active and have enough storage. Transfers that run longer than 36 hours fail and must be restarted.

    The files land in a transfer folder in the new owner's My Drive. Files in shared drives already belong to the organization and are not deleted with the user.

  2. Step 2. Transfer Calendar data when you delete[1]

    Super admins can transfer Drive files and primary Calendar data in the same delete dialog: next to Data in other apps, choose Transfer, pick the recipient (for example, the manager), and tick the data types.

  3. Step 3. Route their email to a manager[1],[9]

    Google lists two ways to keep a leaver's mail: migrate it to another account, or redirect new messages to another address.

    To redirect, go to Apps, Google Workspace, Gmail, Routing (not Default routing). Under Email forwarding using recipient address map, map the leaver's address to the replacement. Redirected mail is not delivered to the original account.

Reclaim the license

Suspending does not save money. Suspended accounts are billed like active ones.

On the Annual or Fixed-Term plan, deleting an account does not reduce your license count or your bill. The seat is yours to reassign. On the Flexible plan, charges are prorated to the deletion date.

Archiving moves the person to an Archived User license and makes their Workspace license available to reassign within 24 hours.[2],[1],[3]

Timing: what you can still undo

  • Suspend: reversible at any time.
  • Delete: restorable for up to 20 days, and only if a matching license is available and the username has not been reused as an alias or group address.
  • Transfer before you delete. If a transfer fails during deletion, Google stops the deletion and leaves the account untouched.
  • A user on a Vault litigation hold cannot be deleted or have data transferred until the hold is lifted, which can take up to 48 hours to take effect.[4],[1]

Questions

Should I suspend or delete a former employee in Google Workspace?
Suspend on the last day so access stops and nothing is lost. Once Drive, Calendar and mail are handed over, archive the account if you must keep the data, or delete it if you do not.
How long can I restore a deleted Google Workspace user?
Up to 20 days after deletion, according to Google's admin help. You need an available license, and the username must not have been reused.
Does suspending a user sign them out everywhere?
Suspension resets their sign-in cookies automatically and blocks access to services. Google notes that a cookie reset alone does not sign someone out of apps like Gmail and Google Drive for desktop, so suspend rather than relying on the cookie reset.
Does suspending a user stop billing?
No. Google charges suspended accounts at the same rate as active ones. Archive or delete the account to reuse or drop the seat.
What happens to files the person shared with others?
Drive files they own go with the account when it is deleted unless you transfer them first. Files in shared drives belong to the organization and stay.
