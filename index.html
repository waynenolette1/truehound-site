The platform

Every access job, in one product.

Most teams stitch this together from an identity provider, a compliance platform, a SaaS management tool and a spreadsheet. Truehound does it from one inventory, so a leaver found in one place is removed, verified and proven everywhere.

  • People inventory

    Every person and every account, across every app.

    • HR is the authority, your identity provider corroborates
    • Accounts with no person, contractors and service accounts named
    • Last sign-in per account, Unknown when the app hides it

  • Leavers, removed and verified

    Who left, and every account they still have.

    • Caught from HR or an identity provider suspension
    • Offboard one person across every app, with a receipt
    • Repos and keys handed over first; manual steps sent to the app owner or Jira

  • Access reviews and campaigns

    Prepared, shared out, signed, and acted on.

    • Managers or app owners review, with reminders and escalation
    • Recurring and delta reviews, in Slack or Teams
    • Revoke straight from a decision, then re-read to verify
    • Accepted risk carries a reason and an expiry

  • Posture

    Where the risk sits before an auditor finds it.

    • Privileged access across apps: over-privileged people, over-exposed apps
    • Non-human identities with their owners
    • Second factor per account, and which connections stopped working

  • Controls

    The checks your auditor asks about.

    • Separation of duties, flagged on the review line and before a grant
    • Every path revoked: direct, group and role
    • SSO coverage per app, evidence expiry warnings, SIEM export as OCSF

  • Workflows

    Joiners, movers and leavers, without the tickets.

    • A workflow builder with approvals, a dry run and a receipt per step
    • Birthright templates, with a simulator that shows who gains and loses what
    • Apps through identity provider groups, and role mining

  • Discovery and spend

    Every app people use, and what it costs.

    • Shadow IT from OAuth grants, sign-in logs, invoices and a browser extension
    • Contracts and renewals with reminders, duplicate tools flagged
    • Unused licenses reclaimed, verified, with the saving recorded

  • Evidence and operations

    Proof that holds up, and a record of every change.

    • Evidence pack with a hash of every file, a PDF and auditor read-only access
    • Pushed to Vanta or Drata, mapped to SOC 2 and ISO 27001
    • Change history on every rule and template, scheduled reports

  • Assistant

    Ask who has access, and why. Then act.

    • AI features run on your own Anthropic key, added by an admin, billed by Anthropic
    • AI answers from your inventory, with citations and a CSV
    • Requests, revokes and reports from chat, under your approval policy
    • Low-risk access certified by an AI agent; the rest goes to people

  • API, webhooks and SSO

    Fits the way your team already works.

    • A public API and signed webhooks
    • A Terraform provider and an MCP server for your own assistants
    • Single sign-on into Truehound, with admin, reviewer and viewer roles

Security and trust

Built to be trusted with the keys.

A tool that can see who has access has to be careful with it. Here is exactly what Truehound keeps, where, and for how long.

SecurityPrivacyAI disclosure

  • Credentials only if you choose

    A credential is saved only when an admin chooses to, encrypted with a key derived for your workspace, and only its last four characters are ever shown again. The free scan never stores a token.

  • Read-only by default

    Every app connects with read access first. Write access is asked for only when you turn on fixes for that app.

  • Nothing changes without you

    Every removal waits for a person to confirm it. Truehound never decides someone left: your HR system, identity provider or your own list does.

  • Proof you can check

    Every scan and every change is sealed in a SHA-256 hash chain. Verify it yourself on the Proof page, and export it with the report.

  • Clear data retention

    Scan history and the alert log are kept 90 days, sign-in links 24 hours. The proof chain is kept for the life of your workspace, because it is the proof.

  • Secrets stay secret

    A secret found in code is shown as its file, line and a masked preview. The value is never stored or sent anywhere.

FAQ

Before you paste a token.

Something else? A person reads every email to truehoundhq@truehound.io.

Where do my app credentials go?
Nowhere unless an admin chooses to save one. A saved credential is encrypted with a key derived for your workspace, only its last four characters are ever shown again, and you can remove it on Setup at any time. The free scan never stores a token.
How do you know who left?
Your HR system or identity provider says so: a leaving date in BambooHR, Rippling or Deel, or an account suspended in Google Workspace, Okta or Entra. With neither connected, you add the list yourself. Truehound never decides on its own that someone left.
What can it change?
Only what you approve. Where an app's API allows it, Truehound removes the account or its keys; where it does not, you get the link and the steps, and the next read checks the account is gone. Every removal is recorded in a tamper-evident log.
What does it cost?
Quarter Pack $399 once for one access review. Team $299 a month or $2,990 a year, four reviews a year. Starter $149 a month. Flat, never per seat. The live demo is free. Pricing
What does it do when I am not looking?
If you choose to store a read-only GitHub token, it is kept encrypted on our server and your organization or account is re-scanned every 6 hours. The results update in the app. It does not send email or Slack alerts yet, and it never removes anyone without your approval.
Can I use it for a SOC 2 access review?
Yes, as evidence. A review lists every account in every connected app with a decision, a reason, the reviewer and the date, and the evidence pack adds the population, the revokes and how each one was verified, with a hash of every file. Your auditor decides what it covers.
Is this a full security program?
No. Truehound checks the access your connected apps show it. It is not a substitute for a full security program and it does not prevent every breach. It closes the gap that the cases above came through: access nobody took back.
