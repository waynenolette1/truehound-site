Comparison

Truehound vs Corma

Corma is a wide SaaS management tool with a low per-user price and a $0 entry tier. Truehound does one job: find access that should be gone, remove it with approval, and prove it.

The short answer

Choose Corma if you want contracts, licence waste, shadow IT and provisioning in one place, with a very low per-user price. Choose Truehound if the job is the access review itself: a flat price that includes reviews, and a re-read of each app after every removal.

Where Corma is stronger

  • Breadth: discovery, contracts and invoices, licence waste, provisioning, access requests and non-human identity visibility in one product.
  • Integrations it puts at over 200 on its Pro plan, plus browser agents for apps without an API.
  • A $0 entry tier and a low per-user price, with a startup discount.
  • OAuth token revocation, a workflow builder, EU data residency and a Gartner SaaS management recognition.
  • Named customers and a much longer track record. Truehound is new.

Where Truehound is different

  • Access reviews are in the price we publish. At Corma they sit in Pro, which is quoted.
  • Every removal is followed by a re-read of the app; anything still present shows in red, never as done.
  • GitHub depth: outside collaborators, deploy keys, webhooks and secrets in code are their own findings.
  • A flat price that does not grow with headcount.

Questions

Is Corma cheaper than Truehound?
At 50 people its per-user price comes out below our flat price on paper. The comparison changes once you need access reviews, which its pricing table places in the quoted Pro plan.
Does Corma remove access?
Yes: its platform pages describe automated provisioning, offboarding and token revocation. Truehound's difference is the read-back after each removal.
