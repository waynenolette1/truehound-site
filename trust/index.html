02Compliance and reports

  • Not yetCertifications and attestations

    Truehound holds no security certification or attestation. No SOC 2 (Type I or Type II), no ISO 27001, no ISO 27701, no HIPAA attestation, no PCI DSS assessment of our own, and no independent penetration test. Our hosting providers publish their own reports for their facilities; those are theirs, not ours. We will not describe Truehound as certified, compliant or audited until an independent party has issued the report.

    Plan: SOC 2 Type I readiness is the first step, started when a customer needs it. Until then, the questionnaire answers and the evidence on this page are what we can show.

  • Not yetSOC 2 report

    Truehound does not have a SOC 2 report. We do not claim one, and will not until an independent auditor issues it.

    Plan: Start a SOC 2 Type I readiness project when a customer needs it, using the controls on this page as the starting point.

  • Not yetISO 27001

    Not held, and not planned before SOC 2.

  • Not yetIndependent penetration test

    No third party has tested Truehound yet. Our own checks run on every change (see Application security).

    Plan: Commission an independent test before accounts hold customer data in production, and share the summary under NDA.

  • In placeData Processing Agreement

    GDPR Article 28 terms, US service-provider terms, Standard Contractual Clauses, a security annex and a sub-processor annex, published and ready to sign.

  • In placeSecurity questionnaires

    A CAIQ-lite style answer pack is published, every answer drawn from how the product works today, with the file that enforces it. We also answer your own questionnaire directly, usually within 5 business days. Email truehoundhq@truehound.io.

03Data security

  • In placeData stays in your browser by default

    Without an account, tokens, the audit and the workspace are stored in your browser, not on our servers. With an account, your team’s workspace is kept in Cloudflare D1.

  • In placeEncryption in transit

    HTTPS everywhere; plain HTTP redirects to HTTPS. The site accepts only TLS 1.2 and 1.3, with forward secrecy (measured with SSL Labs, see Security grades).

  • In placeEncryption at rest

    App credentials an admin saves, including one-click app sign-ins, are encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key derived for each workspace (HKDF-SHA256) from a master secret held only in the connector’s secret store; connected-app credentials are bound to their workspace and app, and the master key can be rotated. Sessions, invites, API keys and extension keys are stored only as SHA-256 hashes (sign-in link hashes too, once email sign-in is on). Cloudflare encrypts D1 and Workers KV data at rest.

  • In placeWhat we read, and what we never read

    From connected apps: people, accounts, roles, status, dates and grants. Never message, document or email body contents; mailbox discovery is limited to sender, subject and date. Secret values found in code are masked and never kept.

  • PartlyBackups

    Cloudflare D1 keeps point-in-time restore for account data automatically (30 days on a paid Workers plan, 7 days otherwise). Demo-form leads in Workers KV have no backup. We have not yet practised a restore.

    Plan: Run and record a restore drill before accounts hold customer data in production.

  • In placeData retention

    Retention jobs run every 15 minutes in the connector. Scan history and connected-app scans are kept 90 days (the latest successful scan is always kept); sign-in link records 24 hours; sessions 30 days from your last visit; invites 7 days; review links for outside reviewers 120 days; extension sign-ins 395 days; demo-form leads 24 months. The Proof log is kept for the life of the workspace, because it is the evidence. The full table is in the Privacy Policy.

  • In placeErasure

    An owner or admin can export everything a workspace holds as one JSON file; an owner deletes the workspace, and everything stored for it, in one step; a person can delete their account. Retention jobs run every 15 minutes (sign-in records 24 hours, scan histories 90 days, extension sign-ins 13 months, leads 24 months). Restore copies expire within 30 days.

04Application security

  • In placeCredential management

    Our own secrets (payment, email, GitHub App, encryption) live in Cloudflare’s write-only Worker secrets and GitHub Actions secrets, are pushed by the deploy workflow without being printed, and never reach the browser. Tokens you paste are relayed per request and never stored or logged by our connector.

  • In placeSecure development

    TypeScript in strict mode, linting, unit and contract tests, and end-to-end suites. A pre-push gate runs the full validation before code reaches the main branch, the connector deploys only after its own tests run in CI, installs use lockfiles, and GitHub Actions are pinned to exact commits.

  • In placeBrowser hardening

    A strict Content Security Policy (scripts only from our own site; connections only to our site, our connector and the apps you connect), a referrer policy, fonts served from our own site and no third-party scripts.

  • In placeOpen hook script

    The script that governs AI agents' tool calls runs on your developers' laptops, so you can read every line before you install it: it is published as a plain file at /hooks/truehound-hook.mjs (needs Node 18 or newer). It redacts secrets on the laptop before anything is sent, sends one small request, gives up after 250 milliseconds, and reads its token from a config file with mode 600.

  • PartlyVulnerability and patch management

    Dependencies are pinned by lockfile and reviewed by hand when upgraded. Automated dependency alerts are not switched on yet.

    Plan: Switch on GitHub’s dependency alerts and add a dependency audit to CI.

  • In placeResponsible disclosure

    A published disclosure policy with safe harbour, a security.txt file, acknowledgement within 1 business day, and fix targets of 14 days for critical and 30 days for high issues.

05Access control

  • In placeCustomer data access

    Every account query is limited to the workspace it belongs to; a non-member gets “not found”, and automated tests check it. Owner, admin and member roles limit who can change what, and an approval policy can require a second admin.

  • In placeHow Truehound gets access to your apps

    You connect with the narrowest read-only sign-in the app offers (for GitHub, a fine-grained read-only token that you create and can revoke; every call is a read). Nothing is changed in an app until a person approves a fix under your approval policy, where the person who proposed a fix can never approve it, and Truehound then reads the app again to verify. AI is optional and runs on your own Anthropic key (bring your own key): Truehound holds no AI key, pays nothing for your AI, and without your key every feature runs on rules and says so. Saved credentials are encrypted per workspace and are never returned to the browser.

  • PartlyProduction access

    One person, the founder, can reach production (the Cloudflare, GitHub and Stripe accounts). Nobody else has access.

    Plan: Confirm and record two-factor sign-in on every production account, and review access each quarter.

  • PartlyLogging

    Every change, approval and consent in a workspace is written to the Proof log, a SHA-256 hash chain where tampering shows. The connector keeps no request logs and logs errors without personal data.

    Plan: Add retained security logging and alerting for the production accounts.

  • In placePassword security

    Truehound has no passwords to steal: you sign in by payment, an activation code, an invite link or a passkey, and connect apps with their own sign-in. Email sign-in links are built but not switched on yet (no email provider is connected), so no sign-in email is sent today. The session cookie is HttpOnly, Secure and limited to our API.

06Infrastructure

  • In placeHosting

    The website is static files on GitHub Pages, with no server code. The connector and account backend are a Cloudflare Worker, with Cloudflare D1 and Workers KV for storage. We run no servers of our own.

  • In placeData centers

    Physical security is GitHub’s and Cloudflare’s. Each publishes its own SOC 2 and ISO 27001 reports for its facilities.

  • In placeSeparate production

    Production deploys only from the main branch, by CI. Development runs locally on in-memory stores and fixtures, and the public demo uses fictional data only. Customer data is never copied out of production.

  • In placeStatus page

    A public status page shows the connector and site health, with samples kept 100 days. It has no incident feed yet, so incident notices go to affected customers by email. It is not an uptime commitment.

  • PartlyBusiness continuity and disaster recovery

    The site and connector can be rebuilt and redeployed from source, which is kept in two private repositories. There is no uptime commitment unless an order form gives one.

    Plan: Write and test a recovery runbook with target times, alongside the restore drill.

07Corporate security

  • In placeDevices

    Truehound is one person. The founder’s work computer uses full-disk encryption (FileVault, checked 2026-10-22).

  • Not yetSecurity training

    There are no employees to train yet.

    Plan: Security onboarding for every hire before they get any access, and training each year.

  • PartlyIncident response

    The process, in order. Report: anyone can report to truehoundhq@truehound.io, and we acknowledge within 1 business day. Triage: we assess severity within 5 business days. Contain and fix: critical issues get a first fix or mitigation within 14 days and high within 30, per the disclosure policy. Notify: we tell affected customers of a personal data breach by email to their account owners within 72 hours of confirming it. Check the service: the status page shows connector and site health. Learn: the disclosure policy and this page are updated when something changes. Not yet done: a rehearsal of this process.

    Plan: Write the incident runbook and rehearse it once before accounts go live.

10Request documents

Ask for the security overview, policies, DPA or written answers to your questionnaire. To be plain: there is no SOC 2 report and no ISO 27001 certificate to request, because Truehound holds neither today.

Truehound holds no SOC 2 or ISO 27001 report today, so there is none to send. We send what exists: the security overview, policies, the DPA and written answers to your questionnaire. We keep the request for 12 months, then delete it.
